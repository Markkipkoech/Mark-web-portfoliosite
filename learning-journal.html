Learning Journal

Documenting my journey into backend engineering

This journal documents my development toward becoming an independent backend software engineer. It records what I study, what I practise, the problems I encounter and the lessons I take from each stage of learning.

My approach combines structured study, practical exercises, technical research and experimentation. The goal is not simply to complete tutorials, but to understand the principles behind backend systems and gradually develop the ability to solve engineering problems independently.

How I learn

My learning process combines four activities:

  • Structured study: Working through programming, computer science, database and backend engineering concepts in a progressive learning sequence.
  • Practical exercises: Applying concepts through programming tasks, SQL exercises and backend development.
  • Technical research: Consulting official documentation and other learning resources to understand how technologies and engineering practices work.
  • Reflection: Recording what I understood, what challenged me, what failed and what I need to revisit.

My learning roadmap

My structured learning journey covers these areas:

  1. Programming and computer science fundamentals.
  2. Databases and SQL.
  3. Django and Django REST Framework.
  4. Backend architecture and software design.
  5. Authentication and authorization.
  6. Testing and software quality.
  7. Security and reliability.
  8. DevOps and infrastructure.
  9. Advanced backend engineering.

I am progressing through these areas gradually, with an emphasis on understanding and practical ability rather than rushing through topics.

Practical learning and engineering tools

I have developed a Python learning-record utility for my learning repository.

The utility validates lesson information, generates dated Markdown learning records and updates a progress tracker while protecting existing records.

This is also an opportunity to practise Python programming, input validation, file handling and reliable updates to existing data.

Learning philosophy

I believe engineering ability develops through consistent practice, curiosity, careful reasoning and honest reflection.

I want to understand not only how to make something work, but also why it works, where it can fail, what assumptions it depends on and how to verify its behavior.

I treat mistakes and failed attempts as part of the learning process, provided I investigate them and understand what they teach me.

Current learning focus

My current structured learning stage is programming and computer science fundamentals. I am building the foundation needed to approach databases, backend frameworks and more complex engineering concepts with confidence.

What this journal will contain

As I progress, this journal will grow through genuine learning records covering:

  • Topics studied and concepts understood.
  • Practical exercises and implementation attempts.
  • Problems, debugging and failed approaches.
  • Important lessons and corrections.
  • Connections between theoretical concepts and practical engineering.
  • Remaining knowledge gaps and next learning objectives.

Journal entries

Entries will be published as they are genuinely completed and reviewed. Planned work will not be presented as completed work.

The objective

Develop the knowledge, practical experience and independent problem-solving ability required to design, build, test and maintain reliable backend systems.
